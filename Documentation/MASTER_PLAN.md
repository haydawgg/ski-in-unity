## 1. Design target: FEEL

A fast, stylized, physics-based freestyle skiing sandbox.

Loop: ski downhill -> build speed -> carve -> approach a feature -> launch -> spin/flip/grab -> land -> keep flowing -> hit the next feature.

It is NOT a racing game, a realistic ski sim, or a mission-heavy open world. Skiing must feel good even with no scoring.

**Physics philosophy: about 65% believable physics, 35% hidden gameplay assistance. FUN > STRICT REALISM.**
- Real physical principles underneath (gravity, slope, momentum, anisotropic friction, angular momentum).
- Aerial tricks are highly controllable. Spins and flips are big, smooth and readable.
- Landings and rails are forgiving, with subtle help, never obvious, and never rescuing a truly failed trick.
- Failure is cheap: bails are energetic but not brutal, and recovery/retry is nearly instant.

## 5. Physics (highest priority)

Use a **Rigidbody-based skiing system**. NEVER a CharacterController, never `Transform.Translate` downhill, never a canned trajectory.

**Body:** one stable primary Rigidbody (mass about 80 kg, fixed timestep 100 Hz, interpolation on, continuous dynamic collision). Visual skier, skis and poles are child transforms/bones. **Do NOT simulate skis and poles as chaotic independent Rigidbodies.** Ski crossing, pole flail and limb lag are done with procedural animation, IK, and spring-damper transforms (visual only), with limits so nothing clips through the body.

### 5.1 Ski contacts and ground
- Left and right ski each cast (raycast/spherecast, 2 per ski) to get: hit, normal, distance, contact point, surface type (groomed / powder / ice), ski forward and side vectors, edge amount.
- Average into a support surface. The skier conforms to slopes with spring/damper, with no jitter and no instant normal snapping.
- Gravity's slope-parallel component drives acceleration. Steeper = faster, flat = gradual slowdown, uphill = natural speed loss. No fixed artificial speed.

### 5.2 Anisotropic friction (essential)
- LOW friction along the ski's forward axis (about 0.03-0.06 on snow).
- HIGHER lateral grip that scales with edge angle. Flat skis slide, edged skis carve.
- Reduced lateral grip when intentionally skidding.
- Air drag: 0.5 * rho * Cd*A * v^2, reduced when tucked.

### 5.3 Carving and skidding
- Steering sets lean and edge angle. Turn radius comes from ski sidecut and edge angle. Lateral acceleration is v^2/R, capped by grip.
- Low speed = tighter turns. High speed = wider turns. Smooth carving preserves momentum. The skier visibly leans into the turn.
- Exceeding grip = gradual transition to a skid: lateral slipping, speed bleed, rotation, spray, scrape audio.
- **Brake / hockey stop:** skis rotate across the fall line, scrubbing speed with a big spray.
- Tuck: lowers drag, raises top speed. Crouch: lowers center of mass, prepares jumps and trick preload.

### 5.4 Powder
Deeper snow zones: extra drag proportional to speed and depth, softer carving, more spray, skis sink slightly (visual). No per-particle snow simulation.

### 5.5 Jumps
- Launch from the surface normal and incoming velocity, NEVER a fake identical arc. A slow approach gives a smaller jump than a fast one.
- **Pop** (crouch, release at the lip): a moderate speed-dependent vertical impulse that supplements the physical trajectory.
- Input buffering (about 0.1-0.15 s): pop just before the lip, grab just after takeoff, rail input just before contact.

### 5.6 Air (where the game lives)
- Linear momentum conserved (true parabola). Grounded carving forces off.
- Rotation is angular-momentum based on 3 axes: yaw (spin), pitch (flip), roll (off-axis). Input applies torque with low angular damping, so rotation persists and never snaps instantly.
- **Tuck/grab reduces moment of inertia: I = I0 * (1 - 0.35*tuck), so angular velocity increases via L = I*w. Extending slows rotation. It must be clearly noticeable.**
- **Trick preloading:** rotational input shortly before takeoff is stored and applied at the lip. Basic tricks must NOT require preloading.
- Compositional tricks: 180 to 1080+ spins, front/back flips, off-axis, cork-style, spin + flip combos. No per-trick canned animations. Tricks emerge from physics + pose control.
- Style/tweaks that visibly change posture: extend legs, pull knees up, cross skis, bone out a ski, shift the body sideways, tweak a grab.

### 5.7 Landing (very important)
Evaluate at first contact: ground normal, vertical impact speed, facing vs. travel direction (switch landings allowed), body/ski orientation (about 35 degrees alignment tolerance), angular velocity, per-ski contact, landing slope, whether head/torso touches first.
- Tiers: **PERFECT** (speed kept, powder burst) / **CLEAN** (some speed scrubbed) / **SKETCHY** (stumble and recover) / **BAIL**.
- Landing on a transition preserves speed (compression). Landing flat scrubs it.
- **Landing assist (subtle, hidden):** near the end of a jump, if the attempt is reasonably close, damp excess angular velocity, gently align skis to the landing surface, slightly align facing with travel. Good attempts land consistently. Failed tricks still fail.
- **Procedural landing compression:** knee bend scales with impact speed, terrain angle and stance.

### 5.8 Bail / ragdoll
On bail, swap to a ragdoll (13+ bodies, ConfigurableJoints) that inherits linear and angular velocity. Skis may release. Snow burst and slide. Recover/get up in about 2 s or on input. Energetic, not gruesome, fast recovery.

### 5.9 Switch
Track skier orientation vs. velocity: forward/switch takeoff and landing. The player can keep skiing switch after landing.

### 5.10 Quick reset + Session marker
- `RESET`: instant retry to the last safe position.
- `SET MARKER`, then repeated `RESET TO MARKER`: for practicing rails and jumps. Keep the retry delay extremely short.
- Out-of-bounds/fall detection returns the player to a sensible recent position.

### 5.11 Physics tests (required, Unity Test Framework)
- Terminal speed on a 20-degree slope is sane (about 25-40 m/s), and tuck > no tuck.
- A 15 m/s launch off a known kicker gives the expected airtime/distance.
- Angular momentum is conserved in a no-input air rotation.
- Tucking increases spin rate.
- A perfect landing keeps more speed than a flat-ground slap.
- Bail triggers above the crash threshold.
- Also: slope acceleration, surface classification, trick angle quantization, score math, rail path math, landing thresholds, save/load.

## 6. Tricks, grabs, scoring

**TrickTracker** records every air/rail sequence: takeoff speed and stance, airtime, cumulative yaw/pitch/roll (with direction), grab type and duration, tweak amount, rail type/duration/entry and exit rotation, landing stance/quality. Use tolerances (about +/-30 degrees), never exact floats. Generate names ("Cork 720 Safety", "Backflip Mute", "Switch 540 Tail").

**Grabs (minimum):** Safety, Mute, Japan, Tail, Nose, Stale, Blunt, and a Method-style pose, plus criss-cross (crossed skis). Use hand IK so the hands visibly reach the skis. A grab is a real pose, not a label over a static character. Hold time matters.

**Scoring** rewards: rotation size, off-axis, flips, grab difficulty and hold time, tweaks, switch takeoff/landing, rail difficulty/duration, landing quality, feature size, combos. Repeats of the same trick pay less. A bail zeroes the combo.

**Combos:** connected lines continue if the player flows quickly into the next feature (for example 540 Mute -> Down Rail -> 270 Out -> Cork 720).

## 7. Rails

Types: flat, down, round, wide, kink, plus boxes (flat/down/wide/narrow/kink).
- Generated in Blender with **JSON metadata** (rail ID, center path, start/end, width, type) that Unity automatically associates with the imported prefab. Represent grindable paths as splines/sampled paths.
- **Capture:** forgiving, with no exact alignment needed. If the skier is close enough, moving appropriately, and low enough (parallel = 50-50, near-perpendicular = boardslide-style), gently project onto the rail path, preserve momentum, align movement along the rail, and keep the player's body orientation control. It should feel forgiving, not automatic.
- **Balance:** poor orientation raises instability, player input corrects it. Basic rails must never be impossibly hard.
- **Tricks:** spin on, sideways/forward/switch slides, spin/flip out. Exit with a pop and the rail's tangent velocity. Rotation carries into the exit trick and can chain into a combo.
- Effects: snow shavings, metal scrape sound.

## 8. Camera (critical)

Smooth, low third-person follow camera (Cinemachine or custom). NOT a tight over-the-shoulder shooter camera.
- Default: about 6 m behind and 1.8-2.2 m above, slight downward look, spring damping, look-ahead along velocity, anticipating downhill travel. Follows travel direction, never snaps to player rotation.
- FOV about 70-75 normal, 78-84 at high speed. No fisheye. No heavy motion blur (subtle at most, at high speed).
- **Air:** decouple from the skier's rotation, NEVER roll the horizon. Follow with lag, drop lower and tilt up so the skier is framed against the sky and sun on big air, keep the landing zone in view when possible, don't orbit wildly. The trick must stay readable.
- Crash: pull back and orbit slightly. Big landings: tiny shake.
- Obstruction handling against terrain, trees, rails, rocks.
- Stretch: photo mode with a free camera, and a 30 s replay buffer.

## 13. Graphics targets (this must look great)

Stylized, not photoreal: clean geometry, strong readable silhouettes, restrained palette, readable while moving fast.

**Post/rendering:** ACES tonemapping, bloom (threshold about 1.0, intensity about 0.6), SSAO, soft vignette, mild grading, 4 shadow cascades (about 150 m), height/distance fog tinted from the sky (restrained; it must never hide obstacles). Distant mountains get bluish-lavender aerial perspective (lighter and desaturated with distance).

**Sunset preset:** directional light low (8-15 degrees), warm (about 1.0, 0.72, 0.55), sun visible in frame with bloom/flare, lavender-to-pink sky gradient, pink-lit snow with cool violet ambient and shadows, long soft shadows. Don't turn everything orange. Snow still reads as snow.
**Day preset:** sun 35-45 degrees, saturated blue sky, soft stylized cumulus.
**Sky:** custom Shader Graph skybox (gradient + procedural clouds).

**Snow shader (Shader Graph, never flat white):** near-white, slightly cool albedo; fake subsurface/wrap lighting with a warm rim at sunset; two triplanar tiling normal maps for wind ripples; subtle roughness/normal variation; slope response; view-dependent glitter that fades with distance (subtle, performant); slope-based blend to dark triplanar rock on steep faces (above about 50 degrees) with snow-dusted ledges.

**Ski tracks:** each ski leaves its own persistent groove (a scrolling terrain-space heightfield/render texture or trail decals, whichever is most reliable in URP). Follows turns, stops while airborne, deeper trench in powder, fades over time, keeps at least about 200 m of trail.

**Particles:** edge spray (scales with slip and speed), landing powder burst, **crash: large soft flakes across the screen**, rail shavings, optional light falling snow. Airborne = no ground spray. Pool objects and cap particle counts.

**Characters:** matte fabric with slight sheen, strong backlight/rim at sunset.

**Performance:** GPU-instanced trees, LODs, static batching for props, sane shadow distance, object pooling. Profile real gameplay.

## 16. Anti-patterns (do NOT)

- Use a walking character controller, or `Transform.Translate` physics.
- Instantly rotate the character to input direction.
- Give every jump the same scripted arc, or make every landing auto-succeed.
- Require pixel-perfect rail alignment.
- Simulate skis/poles as chaotic independent Rigidbodies during normal play.
- Build the mountain from random noise with no designed runs, or the world from Unity cubes when Blender is available.
- Fill the screen with UI, use photoreal assets, or use copyrighted music/assets.
- Hide bad physics behind better graphics.
- Stop because something merely compiles.

## 17. Acceptance criteria

Not complete until the player can: launch the game, enter the mountain, ski downhill with gravity acceleration, carve left/right, skid, brake, tuck, ride uneven terrain, hit a jump, pop, do 180 / 360 / 540 / 720 / larger spins, frontflips, backflips, off-axis/cork rotations, several grabs and tweaks, land forward and switch, jump onto a rail, slide it, spin off, hit boxes, chain features, set a marker and reset instantly, leave visible ski tracks, produce snow spray, hear dynamic audio, and see the Blender-generated skier, skis, trees, rails, jumps and mountains in a large mountain environment with both lighting presets.

**Game-feel test (the most important, subjective):**
- Does downhill feel good even without tricks? Does carving feel smooth, not like rotating a capsule?
- Does speed feel exciting? Do jumps preserve momentum?
- Can I tell what went wrong after a bad landing?
- Can big tricks be controlled without becoming automatic?
- Are rail entries forgiving? Do tricks chain naturally? Does resetting invite immediate retry?
- Does the camera make tricks look good? Does the mountain offer repeated lines?

If any answer is no, keep tuning.
